# Jurisdiction and available procedural routes

Identify relief sought, parties, subject matter, value, locations, special venue rules, international connections and the current procedural posture. A court already entered in the case is an input, not a verified jurisdiction conclusion.

Examine legal system, court system, subject-matter, territorial and where relevant international jurisdiction independently. Use current [GVG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/gvg/), [ZPO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/zpo/), [ArbGG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/arbgg/), [VwGO](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/vwgo/), [SGG](https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/sgg/) or the applicable specialised statute. For cross-border cases inspect the applicable EU instrument or treaty; do not confuse governing law with international jurisdiction. Verify value thresholds and transition rules against the applicable dated text, rather than a static prompt.

Use [justice directories](https://www.justiz.de/service/verzeichnisse/index.php) and the authority/court's own site for names, addresses and filing information. Distinguish a published email contact from a permitted formal filing channel. Check representation, form, signatures, preliminary proceedings and other admissibility requirements relevant to the chosen route.

Return a comparison of plausible routes with desired result, competent body, legal basis, required evidence, formal requirements, reviewed deadline issue, likely costs/risks and an actionable next preparation step. Mark unresolved jurisdiction facts and competing routes. Do not promise success or file an application.
